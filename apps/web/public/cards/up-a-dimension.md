## How to run
Open the link. Pick a demo, do the real thing with your hands (a water ring, six sticks, a paper strip, a
calculator, a ruler on a pencil), then play the screen version. Ask the question in the yellow box and write
down the child's answer in their own words **before** showing the grown-up answer. "Copy all answers" gives
you the five sentences to paste anywhere.

## Notes
Three demos show "when stuck, add a dimension" (the water ring, six matchsticks, folded paper); two show
scale: twenty doublings pass a million, and a lever multiplies both reach and shake. Each simulation is
exact: the tetrahedron needs a height of 0.82 stick lengths (√(2/3)), the paper keeps its 20 cm length
while the gap closes (chord = 2R·sin(θ/2)), and the lever's far end moves (30 − p)/p times the hand.
The wormhole picture is labeled as math only, never as physics anyone has done.
